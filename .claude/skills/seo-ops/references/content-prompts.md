# Content Prompts

Use the supplied brief, actual source pages, and current search observations. Missing evidence belongs in working notes, not disguised as finished copy.

## Content brief

Inputs: topic, audience, market, reader task, business goal, existing coverage, and source material.

Return:

- Proposed title, description, slug, and H1.
- Search intent and the specific question the article answers.
- Distinct angle and why the operator can support it.
- Section outline with each section's purpose, required facts, and sources.
- Appropriate examples, tables, or diagrams.
- Internal destinations and incoming-link opportunities.
- Optional product integration and CTA tied to the reader's task.
- Evidence gaps, approval requirements, and success measure.

Keep scope proportional to the task. Do not impose a word count, FAQ count, or statistic quota. Hand the approved brief to verified-seo-article-writing for a sourced article.

## Comparison brief

Inputs: alternatives, audience, decision criteria, operator's relationship to the products, market, and current sources.

- Open with a qualified recommendation explaining who each option suits.
- Compare the same criteria for each option.
- Use current primary sources for features, pricing, availability, and restrictions. Record observation dates and relevant plan or region.
- Use "unknown" when evidence is unavailable. Do not treat missing evidence as a product defect.
- Disclose the operator's relationship to a compared product where relevant.
- Include tradeoffs and limits rather than forcing a winner in every row.

| Criterion | Option A | Option B | Source and verification date | Decision impact |
|-----------|----------|----------|------------------------------|-----------------|

Return the comparison structure, evidence table, recommendation, unresolved claims, and a refresh trigger based on volatility. Do not add the current year to imply a review that has not occurred.

## Product-led article

Inputs: approved brief, product facts, permitted sources, audience, and desired next action.

- Answer the reader's question before introducing the product.
- Integrate the product only where its verified capability solves the problem discussed.
- Support examples with real evidence or label them clearly as illustrative.
- Keep takeaways useful to readers who do not choose the product.
- Include a CTA where it naturally supports the next task.
- Do not invent outcomes, testimonials, credentials, or an implied firsthand test.

Return a sourced draft, metadata, relevant visual briefs, and evidence gaps. Use verified-seo-article-writing when full claim verification is required.

## Definition page

Inputs: term, audience, scope, related concepts, and reliable sources.

1. Give a clear standalone definition.
2. Explain why the concept matters to the reader.
3. Describe how it works with an accurate example.
4. Clarify common misconceptions and boundaries.
5. Compare related terms only where confusion is likely.
6. Link to useful related pages that actually exist.

Include expert insight or data only when available and relevant. Return the page, source ledger, and optional markup recommendation after checking current vocabulary and platform docs.

## Expert perspective

Inputs: topic, proposed thesis, actual author experience, evidence, and publication context.

- Distinguish the author's opinion, observation, forecast, and externally established facts.
- Use first-person experience only when supplied or verified.
- Test the thesis against contrary evidence.
- State limitations and what would change the conclusion.
- Offer a practical next step without overstating certainty.

Return the draft, verified author attribution, source notes, and any unresolved claims. Never manufacture a contrarian thesis, original framework, or credential to create authority.

## Direct-answer or snippet rewrite

Inputs: reader question, existing passage, source evidence, and current search display if observable.

- Answer directly using the format that best serves the question.
- Preserve conditions, units, and exceptions.
- Use short sentences and natural wording.
- Include a number only if it helps and is supported.
- Avoid hooks that weaken the answer or promise content the page lacks.

Return the rewritten block, rationale, source check, and any observed display context. Do not promise a snippet or citation. Check current snippet-control guidance before suggesting a directive change.

## Internal-link plan

Inputs: new or existing article, canonical URL, topic, and accessible site inventory.

Return both directions:

| Direction | Source URL | Destination URL | Anchor | Exact insertion context | Reason | Status |
|-----------|------------|-----------------|--------|-------------------------|--------|--------|

Use internal-linking for the full workflow. Verify destinations, read source passages, and choose links that help the reader. Do not force sibling links, invent insertion quotes, or substitute a sitemap listing for a page check.

## Content refresh

Inputs: page, prior update date, target intent, performance baseline, and available sources.

| Element | Verified issue | Evidence | Proposed change | Effort | Expected reader benefit |
|---------|----------------|----------|-----------------|--------|-------------------------|

Inspect facts, screenshots, links, product or market changes, search intent, query mix, schema, rendering, and relevant performance signals. Separate observed decay from a proposed explanation.

Preserve accurate valuable content. Update only what the evidence justifies. Do not change the displayed date as a cosmetic freshness tactic. Return prioritized edits, retained content, source changes, a rollback record, and a review date.
