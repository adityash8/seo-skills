# Vendor Trade-Show Release Verification

Use for manufacturer, software, machinery, or supplier news tied to a trade show.

## Separate four claims

1. **Event:** verify the edition, dates, venue, organiser, co-location, and current status on official event pages.
2. **Attendance:** distinguish an announced booth, an official directory entry, and a post-show report.
3. **Showcase:** require evidence tied to that exact edition and product. A pre-event announcement supports a plan; a post-show account supports what the company says it demonstrated.
4. **Product existence:** a catalogue page proves the product is listed, not that it appeared at the current event.

## Pitfall: mixed editions and sources

A release can blend an earlier edition, a similarly named event, a general portfolio page, pre-event marketing, and post-event language.

Build an event/date/source map before drafting. Do not merge those sources into one account of a completed showcase.

## Match wording to evidence

| Evidence | Supported wording |
|---|---|
| Pre-event company announcement | The company announced plans to exhibit. |
| Official exhibitor directory | The company was listed as an exhibitor. |
| Post-show company release naming products | The company said it demonstrated the named products. |
| Product catalogue only | The products are in the company's catalogue; attendance is unconfirmed. |

## Claims needing extra checks

- Successful demonstrations, sales results, and visitor response require evidence from after the event.
- Distinguish forecast counts from final counts. Attribute organiser-reported attendance, floor space, and commercial outcomes even in a final report.
- Check that executive quotations belong to the same event and edition.
- A stale organiser page supports scheduled facts, not final outcomes.
- An association's affiliation with another body does not establish who operates the event.
- Resolve conflicting figures by date, authority, and scope. Omit unresolved promotional numbers.

## Minimal verification set

1. Official event page for date, venue, and edition.
2. Official exhibitor directory or company booth announcement.
3. Company newsroom search for a post-show release.
4. Exact product name plus the event and edition.
5. Final organiser report for claimed metrics, where available.

If a layer lacks evidence, narrow the wording or omit the claim. Do not fill the gap with a product page from another event.
